# Challenge Brief — Rethinking Recruitment (City of Espoo)

Source: Junction hackathon challenge text, by City of Espoo. Lightly cleaned for typos; content unchanged.

## The question

How can recruitment and matchmaking events organized by the City of Espoo help companies and talent find and connect with each other, now that AI-generated job search materials and AI-assisted recruitment processes are common?

## Context

- Finding the right talent is harder, especially in fast-changing industries and international recruitment.
- Professionals struggle to communicate what they can actually contribute; companies struggle to articulate what they really need.
- Espoo runs recruitment and matchmaking events online, in person, and hybrid. Event organizing is strong; facilitating the *right* matches between companies and jobseekers is the weak spot.
- Opportunity: rethink how talent, skills, needs and opportunities are identified, described, communicated and matched.

## The core problem

- Events run on incomplete information.
- Companies describe their talent/service needs poorly, and **late** — often just before the event day.
- Professionals don't know how to communicate strengths and potential.
- CVs, applications and online profiles don't give an accurate picture of either side.
- Both sides increasingly interact through **machine-generated interpretations** (AI-written CVs read by AI screeners) rather than genuine understanding.

## Scope

Prototype new ways to help companies and talent find relevant, efficient, meaningful connections at Espoo events. Any stage is in scope:

1. **Before** — registering, describing needs/skills, pre-event preparation
2. **During** — attending, finding the right conversations
3. **After** — follow-up
4. Or the whole journey end to end

## Known pain points (from Espoo)

| # | Pain point | Who feels it | Stage |
|---|-----------|--------------|-------|
| 1 | Companies are hard to engage: they don't see the city (public employment services) as a talent pipeline, assume no suitable candidates in the city's jobseeker pool, and question quality because the service is free | Company, Organiser | Before |
| 2 | Companies struggle to describe actual talent/service needs clearly and early enough | Company, Organiser | Before |
| 3 | Organisers get many applications but can't see applicants' real qualities and potential or how they match company needs — especially for tech skills | Organiser | Before |
| 4 | Companies get overwhelmed by large volumes of largely irrelevant candidate approaches during events | Company | During |
| 5 | Professionals find it hard to describe competencies, transferable skills and value proposition in a way that gets recruiter attention | Talent | Before / During |
| 6 | AI-written CVs analysed by AI screeners → matching on synthetic representations, not genuine capability and need | All | All |
| 7 | Online event platforms with matching algorithms feel cumbersome | All | During |
| 8 | Online event platform may crash when everyone logs in at event start | All | During |
| 9 | International recruitment adds authority compliance, language, culture, relocation and onboarding complexity | Company, Talent | Before / After |

## What Espoo wants

- **Stronger signal, less noise** throughout registration and attendance; help both sides focus on the most relevant opportunities and conversations.
- No reinforcing of bias; no systematic disadvantage by nationality, language, educational background, career path or other characteristics unrelated to the opportunity.

## Hard rules

See [constraints.md](constraints.md). Summary: synthetic data only, minimal personal data, EU AI Act compliance story, **no automated profiling or automated decision-making about individuals without human involvement**.

## Support from Espoo

- Subject-matter experts: companies, talent attraction, international recruitment, event organizing
- Example scenarios and user journeys from real events
- Feedback on concepts from organiser, company and talent perspectives
- Guidance on responsible AI, privacy and public-sector considerations

**Mentors:** City of Espoo International Working Life Services and Business Services teams — on-site and on Discord.
**Main contact:** Melissa Arni-Hardén, Client Relations Manager & International Working Life Services team lead.

## Judging

Solutions that improve matching, discovery and communication between companies and talent. Teams should consider privacy, transparency, fairness and inclusion; avoid unnecessary personal data processing; not rely on automated decision-making about individuals. Any technology is allowed — the focus is on solving the underlying problem.
