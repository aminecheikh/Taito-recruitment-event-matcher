# 04 — Recruitment practice and I/O psychology evidence

Verification note: figures below come from web search result summaries, mostly secondary. Items I could not confirm in a primary source are marked *(unverified)*. The OECD Finland page and the Espoo TalentMatch page were the only pages opened directly (the OECD fetch returned 403, so OECD points rely on search-result excerpts).

## 1. Key takeaways

1. **Structure beats impression.** In both the classic (Schmidt & Hunter 1998) and revised (Sackett et al. 2022) meta-analyses, structured interviews are among the best predictors of job performance. Unstructured interviews are much weaker (about .19 in the 2022 revision *(secondary source)*). A short event conversation is only useful if it is structured.
2. **Sackett 2022 lowered most validities by .10-.20** but kept the ranking: structured interview first at about .42 *(HumRRO summary)*. Experience and CV signals rank well below structured methods.
3. **Dropping degree requirements on paper changes little in practice.** HBS/Burning Glass (2024): +3.5 percentage points of non-degreed hires in roles that dropped requirements; 45% of firms "in name only". Process change, not wording change, is what matters. This supports forcing recruiters to rank requirements into a real top 4.
4. **Long requirement lists and masculine-coded wording deter some applicants** (Gaucher et al. 2011, mainly via lower "belonging"). The popular "men apply at 60%, women at 100%" claim is weakly sourced *(unverified origin)*.
5. **Candidates want feedback and closure.** Vendor surveys: about 70% want post-interview feedback; 79% would reapply if given it (Greenhouse 2024). Numbers are vendor-run, so treat them as directional.
6. **Finland's barriers for international talent are language requirements, network hiring, few internships, and fragmented services** (OECD 2025; UEF/Turku research). Name-based discrimination is documented in Finnish field experiments.
7. **Espoo TalentMatch** events are large relative to the time available: 200-1,000 job seekers and 5-50 employers per event, with 1:1 meetings (Espoo). Matching, not attendance, is the bottleneck.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Validity of selection methods
- Schmidt & Hunter (1998), "The Validity and Utility of Selection Methods in Personnel Psychology", Psychological Bulletin 124(2). Search summaries list work sample .54, structured interview .51, unstructured interview .38 (corrected validities). Years of experience figure (.18 as I recalled it) was not confirmed *(unverified)*. Source: https://web.pdx.edu/~mccunee/quant_621/Outlines/Schmidt%20&%20Hunter%20(1998).doc
- Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens (2022), "Revisiting Meta-Analytic Estimates of Validity in Personnel Selection", J. Applied Psychology 107(11), 2040-2068, doi:10.1037/apl0000994. Argues range-restriction corrections were often inappropriate; most validities drop by .10-.20 (work samples about -.21, unstructured interviews about -.19). Structured interview about .42, unstructured about .19 (secondary: HumRRO https://www.humrro.org/?p=5963).
- Sackett et al. (2023), "Revisiting the design of selection systems...", Industrial and Organizational Psychology 16(3), doi:10.1017/iop.2023.24, draws out practical implications.
- I did not obtain the 2022 work-sample number *(unverified)*.

### 2.2 Structured interview design
- Levashina, Hartwell, Morgeson & Campion (2014), "The structured employment interview: narrative and quantitative review", Personnel Psychology 67, 241-293. Structure reduces the influence of biasing factors such as race, gender and disability on ratings. Effect sizes not confirmed *(unverified)*. https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/manage_facpub/423/
- Trade-off noted in the same review: Chapman & Zweig (2005) found applicants had lower intentions to accept an offer when structure was high. A fully rigid script can feel cold, so design for warmth inside a fixed question set.
- US OPM guidance describes standard elements: same questions for all, anchored rating scales, multiple raters. https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/other-assessment-methods/structured-interviews/

### 2.3 Skills-based hiring
- Fuller & Raman (2017), "Dismissed by Degrees", Accenture / Grads of Life / HBS. 26 million postings analysed, 600 business and HR leaders surveyed; about 6.2 million US jobs at risk of "degree inflation" (secondary). The specific 67% production-supervisor statistic I recalled was not confirmed *(unverified)*.
- Sigelman, Fuller & Martin (2024), "Skills-Based Hiring: The Long Road from Pronouncements to Practice", Burning Glass Institute / HBS. 11,300 roles at large firms; +3.5 pp non-degreed hiring where requirements were dropped; fewer than 1 in 700 hires affected in 2023; 45% of firms "in name only", 18% "backsliders"; at leader firms non-degreed workers had 10 pp higher retention. https://burningglassinstitute.org/research/skills-based-hiring-2024
- LinkedIn/WEF/OECD skills-based-hiring reports: not retrieved in this pass *(unverified, not cited)*.

### 2.4 Requirement inflation and job-ad quality
- Gaucher, Friesen & Kay (2011), JPSP 101(1), 109-128. Masculine-coded wording appeared more in male-dominated fields (97% vs 70% of ads in the archival sample, per summary); experiments showed it lowered women's anticipated belonging and interest, not perceived skill. Participants did not notice the wording. A replication found the effect only in start-up contexts (Seong & Parker, secondary). https://gap.hks.harvard.edu/evidence-gendered-wording-job-advertisements-exists-and-sustains-gender-inequality
- Tara Mohr (2014), HBR: survey of 1,000+ professionals; lack of confidence was the least-cited reason for not applying (about 10% of women, 12% of men); 15% of women vs 8% of men said they followed the stated guidelines. The "HP internal report" 60%/100% claim is secondhand and may concern internal promotion *(unverified)*. https://www.nextavenue.org/are-women-too-timid-when-they-job-search/
- Direct evidence on "must-have vs nice-to-have" framing was not found in this pass *(gap)*.

### 2.5 Candidate experience
- Greenhouse 2024 Candidate Experience Report (vendor): 79% would reapply if given feedback; about 70% want feedback (HR Brew coverage). Ghosting prevalence quoted as 75% (one write-up) and 67% (SHRM summary) — conflicting. https://www.greenhouse.com/uk/blog/2024-greenhouse-candidate-experience-report
- LiveCareer survey (1,000+ US workers, vendor): 48% would not reapply after being ghosted. https://www.livecareer.com/resources/careers/ghosting-consequences
- HireVue cites a survey where 62% lose interest after two weeks of silence *(secondary, unverified)*.
- Nothing found on job-fair-specific candidate preferences *(gap)*.

### 2.6 International recruitment in Finland
- OECD (2025), Economic Surveys: Finland, chapter "Enriching human capital with more foreign talent": Talent Boost and streamlined permits are a solid base, but "restrictive language requirements, limited internship opportunities and professional networks for foreign students and insufficient integration support" hinder participation and retention; no numerical targets currently in place. https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-finland-2025_985d0555-en/full-report/enriching-human-capital-with-more-foreign-talent_5745d827.html (excerpt via search, page not opened)
- UEF / University of Turku research (reported by UEF): talent services are fragmented, hard to use, providers lack skills-recognition expertise; migrants feel expertise is undervalued because jobs demand Finnish and personal networks. https://www.uef.fi/en/article/finland-attracts-international-talent-but-struggles-to-retain-it
- Work in Finland (2021 employer survey, cited): international recruitment seen as burdensome and complex; language is the main worry. https://workinfinland.com/tyonantajat/suunnittele/kansainvalisyydesta-kasvua
- Discrimination: Ahmad's correspondence study, about 5,000 applications in 2016-17 with Finnish, English, Iraqi, Russian and Somali names; ethnicity mattered in callbacks and contact order (effect sizes unconfirmed). Finnish Institute of Occupational Health survey: 39% of HR professionals admitted foreign-sounding surnames lowered the chance of a callback or hire (self-report, secondary). Jyväskylä 2025 (Kiviholma): anonymous recruitment helped applicants with foreign-sounding names, but only when mandatory. Source list in 5.
- Espoo context: TalentMatch is a free, English-language series with online or in-person formats, industry-specific or open, 1:1 meetings, pitching and keynotes; 200-1,000 job seekers and 5-50 employers per event. https://www.espoo.fi/en/talentmatch-events . Employer Speed Dating targets student recruitment; Entrypoint Mentoring serves unemployed international professionals (Espoo pages via search).

### 2.7 Why employers disengage from public services
- No rigorous study found on employer disengagement from public matchmaking events *(gap)*. The nearest evidence is the UEF/Turku finding of fragmented services and the Work in Finland employer survey on complexity. Signal quality and irrelevant approaches are plausible but **not evidenced in my sources** *(unverified; validate by interviewing Espoo staff and employers)*.

## 3. So what for our design

1. **Make the 10-minute conversation a mini structured interview.** Fixed order: the recruiter's top 4, one question each (about 2 minutes), then 2 minutes for open questions. Same four questions for every candidate that recruiter meets. This is the interview format with the highest validity evidence.
2. **Behavioural, example-based questions.** Candidates prepared real examples per requirement; ask "tell me about a time you did X". This approximates a work sample or past-behaviour method, which outperforms CV claims.
3. **Anchored 1-5 rating card per requirement, filled by the recruiter after the talk.** Behavioural anchors (e.g., "named own role, concrete result, transferable context"). Ratings stay with the human; the system makes no automated decision, which fits the EU AI Act constraint.
4. **Frame the top 4 as a forced ranking.** The skills-based-hiring evidence shows stated requirements and actual practice diverge. Forcing a top 4 plus a short "everything else is a bonus" list makes the real criteria visible. Collect it before the event with a hard deadline, as late input is a named pain point.
5. **Use plain, non-gendered, non-credential wording** in the four requirements. Offer a simple wording check (flag masculine-coded words, degree-only phrases). Display requirements as "what you will do" rather than credentials.
6. **Shared requirements in advance reduce the disclosure problem for international candidates**, who struggle to communicate strengths: they can prepare, in their own words, evidence of transferable skills. Language-level expectations should be stated explicitly per requirement, since language is the OECD-named barrier.
7. **Close the loop.** Every candidate gets a short outcome note from each recruiter met, and ideally one line of feedback. This addresses ghosting evidence.
8. **Caution:** high structure can lower applicant warmth (Chapman & Zweig 2005). Keep the opening 30 seconds free-form.

## 4. Finland-specific facts for a pitch

- OECD 2025: Finland's own review names restrictive language requirements, limited internships and networks as barriers to foreign talent, and notes no numerical Talent Boost targets are currently in place.
- UEF/Turku: international talent services are described as fragmented and hard to use; migrants feel their expertise is not valued because jobs require Finnish and personal networks.
- Finnish Institute of Occupational Health survey (secondary): 39% of HR professionals said a foreign-sounding surname reduced hiring or callback chances.
- Ahmad correspondence study: about 5,000 fictitious applications; ethnicity affected callbacks and order of contact.
- Espoo TalentMatch scale: 200-1,000 job seekers vs 5-50 employers per event, a ratio of up to about 20 job seekers per employer at the upper end (our arithmetic from Espoo's ranges).
