# 02 - The Science of Miscommunication

Scope: why candidates and recruiters fail to understand each other. Method: web search and fetch on 2026-11-14. Search quota was intermittently throttled, so several topics rest on abstracts and secondary summaries; these are flagged. Items marked *(unverified)* were not confirmed against a source we opened.

## 1. Key takeaways

1. **Interview impressions track self-presentation more than job performance.** Meta-analysis (Barrick et al. 2009) reports impression management correlates r = .47 with interview ratings but r = .15 with job performance; unstructured interviews are most affected. A conversation at an event is a short unstructured interview, so it inherits this problem.
2. **Structure is the best-evidenced countermeasure.** Structured interviews weaken the influence of race, gender and disability on ratings (Levashina et al. 2014), and the accent meta-analysis authors recommend predefined, job-focused questions. Our "talk about the 4 core things" format is a structured interview in spirit.
3. **Self-evaluation, not just self-promotion, is the root of undersell.** Exley & Kessler (2022) find women rate their own performance lower than equally performing men; the gap persists when there is no incentive to promote. Prompts must help people recall facts, not "be confident".
4. **Accent and non-standard speech are read as lower competence.** A 2024 meta-analysis (22 studies, 3,615 participants) finds non-standard speakers rated less competent and less hirable with identical answer content; HR professionals are not immune. This is the direct mechanism by which language fluency gets confused with skill.
5. **Discrimination by name is large, persistent and documented in Finland.** Finnish correspondence experiments (Ahmad 2020) show fewer interview invitations for immigrant-named applicants, worse for non-European origins. Cross-national meta-analysis shows no decline in the US over 25 years.
6. **Credentials are discounted when employers cannot verify them.** Evidence points to statistical discrimination under imperfect information about foreign credentials; assessment/recognition recovers part of the penalty. Specific, concrete evidence of skill substitutes for a trusted credential.
7. **Requirement inflation is real and posting text does not equal real need.** Degree requirements appear in postings that the actual hiring then does not honor (Burning Glass/HBS 2024). Asking recruiters to rank a top 4 attacks the wishlist directly.
8. **The "women apply only at 100% vs men at 60%" statistic is not evidenced.** The HP source cannot be located; better data show a small gap (52% vs 56%). Do not cite it in our product or pitch.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Signaling and information asymmetry
- Spence (1973), "Job Market Signaling", *QJE* 87(3), 355-374 (http://www.jstor.org/stable/1882010). Employers cannot observe productivity; applicants send signals (education). A signal is credible when it is too costly for low-ability types to imitate. Even a signal with no productivity effect can sustain an equilibrium.
- Akerlof (1970), "The Market for Lemons", *QJE*: adverse selection under asymmetry. *(from memory; not opened)*
- Connelly, Certo, Ireland & Reutzel (2011), "Signaling Theory: A Review and Assessment", *J. of Management* 37(1), 39-67, doi:10.1177/0149206310388419. Frames signaler/receiver; a 2024 update stresses that a signal's power lies in its cost.
- Implication: an AI-polished CV is a near-zero-cost signal, so it has lost credibility (cheap talk). A signal that is costly to fake in a live setting (a specific, checkable story, answered to follow-up questions) is credible. *(Our inference from the theory, not a tested finding.)*

### 2.2 Impression management and who undersells
- Barrick, Shaffer & DeGrassi (2009), "What You See May Not Be What You Get", *J. Applied Psychology* 94, 1394-1411, doi:10.1037/a0016532. Meta-analysis; IM r = .47 with interview ratings vs .15 with performance (figures as cited by later work).
- Levashina, Hartwell, Morgeson & Campion (2014), "The Structured Employment Interview", *Personnel Psychology* 67(1), 241-293, doi:10.1111/peps.12052. Structure reduces demographic influence on ratings (from summaries; full text not read). Peck & Levashina (2017, *Frontiers in Psychology*, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00201) report self-promotion r = .26 with structured interview ratings *(figure via search summary)*.
- Exley & Kessler (2022), "The Gender Gap in Self-Promotion", *QJE* 137(3), 1345-1381, doi:10.1093/qje/qjac003. Experiments with 4,000+ adults and 10,000+ youths; gap persists without promotion incentives and appears by grade 6.
- Culture: we found no peer-reviewed study that measures Finnish modesty against interview outcomes. Practitioner sources (Metropolia blog) describe equality/modesty norms and direct, brief communication. Treat "Finnish modesty" and high/low-context or "I vs we" framing as plausible but *(unverified empirically)* for hiring outcomes.
- Neurodiversity and class: *(not researched; no sources opened)*.

### 2.3 Accent, fluency and competence
- Schulte, Basch, Hay & Melchers (2024), meta-analysis, *Applied Psychology*, via FU Berlin release (https://fu-berlin.de/en/presse/informationen/fup/2024/fup_24_084-metastudie-dialekte-akzente-bewerbungsverfahren/index.html). 22 studies, 3,615 participants; non-standard varieties (regional, ethnic/migration) rated less competent and less hirable with identical content; HR professionals not less biased; effect not reduced by non-standard interviewers. Effect sizes not stated on the page.
- Related single studies surfaced in search (not opened): foreign accents and employment decisions (Emerald, doi 10.1108/02683941011019339); Timming, foreign accent in hiring (St Andrews repository).
- Toronto resume field experiment: recruiters cited language concerns for discounting foreign experience *(secondary summary; study not identified)*.

### 2.4 Recruiter cognition and discrimination
- Ambady & Rosenthal (1992), *Psychological Bulletin* 111(2), 256-274: thin slices under 5 minutes predict outcomes (mean r ~ .39 per a summary). Cuts both ways: quick judgments are partly valid but are also where accent, appearance and similarity enter. Similarity-attraction, halo, confirmation bias: *no primary source retrieved*.
- CV skim time (e.g., "6 seconds"): not verified; do not cite.
- Bertrand & Mullainathan (2004), *AER* 94, NBER w9873: white-sounding names got ~50% more callbacks; higher-quality CVs helped white applicants far more than Black applicants.
- Quillian, Pager, Hexel & Midtboen (2017), *PNAS*: meta-analysis of 28 experiments; whites got 36% more callbacks than Black applicants and 24% more than Latinos; no decline over 25 years for Black applicants. Extended in *Sociological Science* 2019 (97 experiments, 9 countries).
- Ahmad (2020), "Do Equal Qualifications Yield Equal Rewards for Immigrants in the Labour Market?", *Work, Employment & Society* 34(5), 826-843, doi:10.1177/0950017020919670. 5 applicants (Finnish, English, Iraqi, Russian, Somali names), 5,000 applications to 1,000 vacancies; immigrants got significantly fewer interview invitations at equal qualifications; non-European origin worse. Companion papers: Ahmad (2020), *Sociological Inquiry* 90(3), 468-496 (European-name preference over non-European); *European Societies* 22(5), 659-681, doi:10.1080/14616696.2020.1822536 (second generation; also contact order). Per-group callback rates not retrieved.
- Bohnet, van Geen & Bazerman (2016), *Management Science* 62(5), 1225-1234, doi:10.1287/mnsc.2015.2186: gender influenced choices under separate evaluation but not joint evaluation (comparing candidates side by side on performance).

### 2.5 Tacit knowledge, transferable skills, foreign credentials
- Tacit knowledge (Polanyi) and difficulty articulating skills: *no source opened*; flagged as a well-known concept but unverified here.
- Damelang, Ebensperger & Stumpf (2020), *Social Forces*: vignette experiment; recognized foreign credentials narrow but do not close the hiring gap (https://cris.fau.de/publications/231202861).
- IZA DP 9597 "Local signals and the returns to foreign education" (later *Economics of Education Review*, 2017): Australian survey data; credential assessment raises returns, consistent with statistical discrimination from imperfect information.

### 2.6 Employer side: inflation, wishlists, self-selection
- Fuller & Raman (2017), "Dismissed by Degrees", HBS/Accenture/Burning Glass: degree requirements inflated beyond need *(via secondary summary)*. Burning Glass Institute/HBS (Feb 2024), "Skills-Based Hiring: The Long Road from Pronouncements to Practice": 11,300 roles at large firms; 45% of firms dropped degree requirements from postings with little change in hiring (https://www.burningglassinstitute.org/s/Skills-Based-Hiring-02122024-vF-srmp.pdf, listed in search, not opened).
- "Purple squirrel": term is practitioner jargon; no peer-reviewed source found.
- HP statistic (women apply at 100% vs men 60%): traced to an HP internal report that no one has located; popularized by Mohr (HBR 2014) and *Lean In*. Mohr's own survey (1,000+ respondents) gave reasons such as fear of failure and "following the rules". Behavioural Insights Team (2022), n > 10,000 online experiment: men apply at 52.1% of requirements met, women 55.7%; LinkedIn (2019) report: women 16% less likely to apply after viewing, though more likely to be hired when they apply. Conclusion: a gap in self-selection is plausible but small; the 100/60 figures are *(unverified)*. Clear, specific requirements attracted more women to apply (WEF summary of BIT work).

## 3. So what for our design

**Applicant prompts must:**
- Ask for specifics (situation, what *I* did, outcome, evidence) because self-evaluation, not articulation alone, is biased downward; ask follow-up questions that probe "what exactly did you do" so "we" stories convert to "I" contributions without telling people to boast.
- Never write text (cheap-signal risk; also the user rule). Questions only, so what remains is costly-to-fake, in the person's own words.
- Be language-tolerant: allow the person's native language drafting or notes, and accept plain wording; accent/fluency penalty means the written prep should carry competence evidence so recruiters do not infer it from speech.
- Neutral on modesty norms: offer example "good enough" answers in several tones (understated and direct) *(design choice, not evidence-based)*.
- Support credential translation: ask "what did you do with this qualification" rather than naming institution.

**Recruiter ranking must:**
- Force a top 4 with a cost (rank, cannot tie), counteracting wishlist inflation; require early submission as a gate for the event.
- Convert each requirement into a question and a behavioral rubric before the event (structure), and score each of the 4 separately; prefer side-by-side comparison of answers (joint evaluation) to reduce demographic influence.
- Hide name/photo/accent-bearing channels in pre-screening where legal; keep human judgment (no automated profiling).
- Give the recruiter the applicant's evidence per requirement, not a summary score.

## 4. Open questions / contested
- Does structuring a short event conversation preserve interview-structure benefits? Evidence is from formal interviews.
- Effect of IM on ratings (r = .47) mixes honest and deceptive IM and rater bias; causal direction is not settled.
- Cultural modesty effects in Finland lack direct hiring evidence.
- Accent meta-analysis lacks effect sizes in our source; heterogeneity unknown.
- Whether self-selection gaps (HP claim) hold outside the US; the 100/60 figures are unsupported.
- Whether prompts that ask questions still shift content (AI as interviewer leading answers); needs a user test.
- Posting-vs-hiring gap might reflect measurement limits of posting data.
