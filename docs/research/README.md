## Where the reports agree

1. **The input is the weak link, not the algorithm.** Matching platforms fail on thin or late profiles (01). Job fairs produce meetings but few hires because both sides hold wrong beliefs about requirements (Abebe et al., Economic Journal 2025 — 01, 06). Työmarkkinatori notes jobseekers and employers describe skills in different words (01).
2. **Structure beats impression.** Structured interviews predict performance roughly twice as well as unstructured ones (Sackett et al. 2022 ≈ .42 vs .19 — 03, 04). Impression management drives interview ratings (r = .47) far more than job performance (r = .15) (Barrick et al. 2009 — 02). Structure also reduces demographic influence on ratings (Levashina et al. 2014 — 02).
3. **Written materials have lost signal.** After LLMs, a well-written proposal stopped predicting hiring (Galdin & Silbert 2025, working paper — 05). LLM screeners prefer AI-written resumes 67–82% of the time (Xu et al. 2025 — 05).
4. **People undersell for reasons confidence talk can't fix.** Self-evaluation gaps persist without incentives (Exley & Kessler 2022 — 02). Accent lowers perceived competence for identical content, including among HR professionals (2024 meta-analysis — 02). Name discrimination is documented in Finland (Ahmad 2020 — 02).
5. **Employers' stated requirements aren't their real ones.** 45% of firms dropped degree requirements "in name only"; real effect +3.5 pp (HBS/Burning Glass 2024 — 02, 04, 01).
6. **Scarce signals reduce overload.** A small, fixed number of interest signals per applicant makes each one informative and helps non-obvious candidates (Coles, Kushnir & Niederle 2013; Lee & Niederle 2015 — 06).
7. **"Human clicks approve" is not oversight.** People followed a biased AI's picks ~90% of the time under severe bias (UW 2025 — 05). Austria's AMS jobseeker-scoring algorithm is the public-sector cautionary tale (06).
8. **Espoo already runs the nearest thing.** TalentMatch: 200–1,000 jobseekers, 5–50 employers, 15-minute 1:1 intros, meetings on mutual interest; no published outcomes (01, 04).

## Candidate core ideas

Draft principles for the concept, each traced to evidence. To be confirmed with the team.

| # | Principle | Evidence |
|---|-----------|----------|
| P1 | **Upgrade TalentMatch, don't replace it.** Plug into Espoo's existing format. | 01 |
| P2 | **Fix what each side says, early.** The work happens before the event. | 01, 06 |
| P3 | **Recruiters commit to a ranked top 4**, each with one line on what it looks like in practice. | 02, 04 — *untested hypothesis (03)* |
| P4 | **Evidence over eloquence.** Applicants bring one concrete past example per requirement, in their own words. AI asks recall questions; it does not coach "confidence". | 02, 03, 05 |
| P5 | **Scarce interest signals.** Each applicant gets a few signals per event; recruiters see the signal plus the applicant's examples. | 06 |
| P6 | **The conversation is a mini structured interview** around the 4, with a shared card. Written examples on the card offset accent and fluency bias. | 02, 03, 04 |
| P7 | **AI never judges people.** No scores, rankings or "top match" lists for recruiters. AI asks, reflects, and (transparently) fixes language or translates. | 05, 06 |
| P8 | **AI prepares, humans meet.** AI coaching evidence is mixed; Espoo staff coach those who get stuck, and the event itself is human. | 03 |

## Open tensions

- **How much may AI touch the applicant's words?** Resume-polishing help raised hiring ~8% (Wiles et al., Management Science 2025 — 05) and fluency bias penalises non-native speakers, but generated text destroys signal. Possible line: content must be the applicant's; grammar fixes and translation are allowed and labelled.
- **Does ranking work?** No research found on forced prioritisation of requirements. Test it with Espoo mentors.
- **Company engagement (pain point #1)** — no source found on why companies avoid public-sector services.
- **Written examples could anchor recruiters** just as AI recommendations do. Keep the card short and factual.
